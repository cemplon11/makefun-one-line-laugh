# One-Line Laugh — candidates

Bounty: make.fun `u-ronigrv-the-one-line-laugh-7ccca46f` ("The One-Line Laugh").
Judge weighs, in order: funny within 200 chars; a real surprise at the end that
still makes sense; an everyday truth people recognise; economy of words;
freshness. All candidates drafted fresh in this session.

1. My calendar reminded me to relax, so I scheduled worrying about it for Thursday.
2. My smartwatch congratulated me for standing up, which is the only applause I get all day.
3. My phone's screen-time report says I spent six hours on my phone, which I read on my phone.
4. My smart fridge just emailed me a diet plan, so now even my leftovers are judging me.
5. I bought a standing desk to get healthy, and now I just sit on a taller chair.
6. I finally cleaned my inbox to zero, and the first new email was a newsletter about decluttering.
7. My bank called to congratulate me on my savings, then charged me a fee for the call.
8. I told my plants I was going on holiday, and they died out of spite before I packed.
9. My neighbour's dog barks at me every morning, so I've started barking back to keep the peace.
10. I set my alarm for 6 a.m. to seize the day, and the day seized my snooze button instead.
11. My password is so secure even I can't log in, which my bank calls excellent security and I call Tuesday.
12. I meal-prepped for the week on Sunday, and by Monday the containers had unionised against me.
13. I asked my smart speaker for the weather and it told me to look out the window, which is the most honest thing it has ever said.
14. My GPS rerouted me three times and I still arrived late, so now I just blame the satellites in person.
15. The printer only jams when the document is urgent, which is why I print everything in advance and nothing at all.
16. I joined a gym in January and the only thing I have lifted since is the membership card out of my wallet.
17. I told my boss I work best under pressure, so now he just stands behind me all day.
18. I bought noise-cancelling headphones to focus, and now I can't hear my own deadlines screaming.

## Pick

Candidate 1. Cleanest structure: setup (calendar tells me to relax) -> reversal
(scheduled worrying instead) -> everyday truth (we schedule everything, even
relaxation; anxiety about relaxing). Short, economical, fresh, and the twist
lands on the last word.

Final wording (revised in-session): "My calendar just reminded me to relax,
so I've scheduled worrying about it for Thursday." The "just" adds immediacy
and "I've scheduled" keeps it active and spoken; same joke, tighter delivery.
